## 2. Product principles

1. **Discovery precedes a chosen intervention.** Existing prospects, proposals, referral channels, client experience, account expansion, staff development, and automation are subjects to assess. No campaign is the predetermined winner.
2. **Professional playbooks precede local facts.** Supply the directors with practical diagnostic methods and output standards. Learn office-specific conditions from evidence; do not ask Luis to design every department.
3. **Human authority is explicit.** Humans ratify vision and material strategy, approve bounded standing mandates, and retain final communications, commitments and designated accounting decisions. Agents carry out permitted internal work without repeating approvals already granted for that scope.
4. **Evidence stays inspectable.** Each material factual or numerical claim links to an authorized source snapshot, an identified human statement, or a reproducible calculation.
5. **Work survives interruptions.** Store state, evidence, revisions, dependencies, costs, decisions, and completed steps outside chat history.
6. **The critic challenges decisions as well as wording.** It checks whether the proposed priority makes sense, whether capacity exists, and whether the claimed benefit follows from the facts.
7. **Build a small system with complete workflows.** Roles are versioned instructions and capabilities invoked when useful. They do not require separate constantly running services.
8. **Preserve proven work.** Adapt existing client procedures into a durable execution process. Change accounting logic only through a recorded review.
9. **Demonstration and validation remain distinct.** Deterministic fixtures prove software behavior. Live model evaluations, connector checks, and real business results require later gates.
10. **Autonomy must reduce management work.** Measure completed useful work, necessary interventions, review time, missed exceptions and quality. More tasks or fewer visible warnings alone are not evidence of a successful autonomous system.

## 5. Discovery workflow

### 5.1 Establish the mandate

Load the confirmed goals, human authority, company boundary, available sources, operating constraints, and approved budgets. With sparse inputs, start useful analysis and identify the evidence required for stronger conclusions.

The lead must not open with a long questionnaire asking Luis to describe every marketing and sales process. First inspect the material it is authorized to use.

### 5.2 Inventory and map the business

Inventory sources with origin, owner, scope, dates, completeness, and freshness. Build a business map covering:

- Service offering, engagement types, client fit, and approved geography/brand.
- Acquisition/referral channels, commercial stages, documented needs and won/lost reasons.
- Existing client goals, delivery experience, concerns, and expansion needs.
- Workload, due dates, procedures, reviewer demand, skills and coverage.
- Revenue definitions, pricing, scope, labor/tool cost, and operational constraints.
- Existing automation assets and gaps between a written workflow and an executable capability.

Each field is observed, reported, calculated, hypothesized, or unknown. A prior agent summary is a secondary source, with links to its original evidence.

### 5.3 Commission functional investigations

Give each relevant director a complete brief: goal, authorized business/client scope, evidence snapshot IDs, known decisions, exact question, output schema, permitted tools, limits, and dependencies. Independent investigations can run concurrently; dependent writing waits for its inputs.

A director returns findings, strengths to preserve, gaps, competing explanations, opportunities, and the questions that would change a decision. Continue independent work when another function lacks information.

### 5.4 Rank and integrate opportunities

Use transparent priority bands: now, next, investigate, later. Compare business effect, evidence strength, urgency, effort/attention, and readiness with a short rationale. Optional ordinal scores may aid sorting, but must not be converted into invented dollar forecasts.

Apply constraints before ranking: data access, contact restrictions, delivery capacity, required skills, review effort, budget, and dependencies. The strategy lead resolves competing departmental recommendations into a feasible sequence. It identifies deferred alternatives and what new evidence would change the order.

Ask at most three high-value questions in a decision brief by default. This is a presentation default, not a reason to hide material unknowns; keep the full evidence-gap register available.

### 5.5 Produce a decision package

Every material recommendation includes:

1. The problem or opportunity and why it matters now.
2. Supporting facts, dates, source locators, and conflicts.
3. Alternatives, including retaining the current approach when plausible.
4. The recommended action and expected mechanism of benefit.
5. Expected result as a measured outcome or clearly labeled scenario.
6. Required human effort, capacity, cost, dependencies, and uncertainty.
7. Critic findings and their disposition.
8. The complete draft/example when preparation can make the choice more concrete.
9. Exactly what approval authorizes, and the next observable milestone.

Store recommendations and decisions so the lead can explain why a previous choice was made and when to revisit it.

## 7. Critic and revision protocol

Independent review applies to strategy, financial assumptions, capacity, client communication, proposal value, and accounting output. It is not limited to proofreading.

The model-critic protocol below applies where the workflow requires model review. An explicitly approved deterministic accounting workflow may instead use deterministic validation plus an assigned independent human reviewer, with its evidence and disposition saved in the same review records. This exception must be part of the workflow's approved review policy; missing model access never silently changes a required review into a pass.

1. Run deterministic validation of schema, calculations, evidence identities, scope, and required fields.
2. Give the critic the original task, success criteria, policy, original source snapshots, calculations, and candidate artifact.
3. Return pass, revise, or escalate. Each finding includes severity, affected claim/action, evidence, required correction, and whether it blocks release.
4. The producer creates a new artifact version with a change map. The critic does not edit the producer's draft or its source records.
5. Allow a maximum of three reviews total: the initial review plus two producer-revision reviews. Enforce this in application code.
6. At the limit, preserve the available work and unresolved findings for Luis. Never turn an unresolved failure into a pass to finish the workflow.

The reviewer may create a separate review record through a restricted endpoint; it has read-only access to evidence and producer artifacts. It cannot grant approvals or modify policy.

A critical unresolved issue prevents the affected artifact entering an action-ready state. A human can request revision, provide evidence, or make a recorded business decision within actual authority; they cannot use an override to defeat data-scope restrictions, disabled capabilities, or an invalid accounting payload.

Critic pass is a review result, not human authorization or a guarantee of correctness. Different models are an optional evaluation choice; original-source checks and measurable defect detection determine reviewer effectiveness.

## 8. Approval and action policy

### Types of authorization

| Type | What it covers | What it does not authorize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal mandate | Defined research, analysis, drafting and task coordination within scope/budget | New data access, external communications, accounting writes |
| Standing operating mandate | Versioned permission for specified recurring/internal work, triggers, clients, actions, limits, review rules and validity period | New strategic direction, broader permissions, external commitments or bypassing exact action approvals |
| Strategy approval | A versioned initiative, targets, preparation scope, effort and budget | Sending the resulting drafts or changing a client's books |
| Final communication review | Exact sender, recipients, channel, subject/body and attachment versions for human use | Automatic sending, changed content or a different recipient |
| Accounting action approval | A specific supported client/account/period/amount/payload or later approved routine rule | Unusual/material adjustments outside that scope or final close |
| Final close sign-off | The reviewed client-period package and evidenced completion of required steps | Retroactive authorization of undocumented actions |
| Connection or policy approval | A particular integration, capability, scope or budget configuration | Unrelated systems, clients, tools or spending |
| Company release approval | A tested release, environment, pilot cohort, client/workflow scope and budget | Automatic broader rollout, additional actions or a transfer of existing approvals |

A human's approval is stored through an authenticated endpoint. AI agents cannot invoke that endpoint as human approvers. A natural-language statement that approval exists is insufficient.

An active standing mandate can authorize the next internal work item without a new human click. Trusted code evaluates the action against the mandate's scope, current policy, preconditions, budget, expiry and revocation. Persist that authorization decision. The mandate is an approved policy record, not a generic approval attached to every artifact. Exact-version approval still applies to communications, consequential actions and other designated outputs. Creating a new draft does not by itself revoke an unrelated standing mandate; relevant scope or source changes require revalidation.

New or materially changed strategy, clients, services, budget limits, commitments and capabilities require their applicable human decision. Human silence, an unanswered alert, a model's claimed authority, and the critic's pass never grant approval. An expired/revoked mandate pauses affected tasks, while independent authorized work continues.

Legal review is a recorded assessment or human/counsel disposition, not signing authority. A critic pass, Legal review completion or company sponsorship cannot authorize contract execution or another restricted action. Contract signatures, vendor-term acceptance, rights waivers and dispute settlements remain outside agent tools.

Approval records bind to organization/client scope, human actor, action type, payload/artifact hash, source preconditions, policy version, creation time, expiry and revocation. Relevant edits invalidate the affected approval. Recheck immediately before any authorized action.

M0-M7 contain no automatic send/publish/payment/delete tools. They export complete drafts for human sending. A manual send is recorded as human-reported unless independently verified by a provider event. Do not label a draft delivered.

Later routine accounting execution is optional M8 work. It requires a client-specific rule, proven connector capability, tested preconditions, recovery design and explicit authority. Approval to build the plan or connect read access does not activate it.

## 10. Operating modes and provider contract

### Modes

- **Demo:** synthetic data and a deterministic provider, with zero model-provider or business-connector calls. Local demo authentication is allowed only in the local environment; a hosted demo uses real company authentication and only the identity/infrastructure traffic required for that hosting.
- **Evaluation:** explicitly authorized provider and supplied approved samples, with external effects disabled and outputs assessed against a human-reviewed reference.
- **Connected pilot:** configured identity, verified read capabilities, company/client scopes, budget and operational owner. Start with read access and draft preparation.

Switching modes never relabels existing artifacts. Each artifact keeps its source mode and run provenance. If a live provider fails, return an error/blocked state; do not substitute a fixture response.

### Environments are separate from operating modes

Use explicit deployment environments local, staging and production. Environment identifies where the application runs; mode identifies the provider/data/action configuration. For example, local evaluation may call an authorized model before any cloud hosting, and company staging may run either synthetic demo cases or approved evaluation samples. No environment name enables capabilities.

Keep each hosted environment's database, credentials, callback URLs, session signing material, storage, logs, queues and schedules separate. Hosted staging requires real authentication and company access controls even if its inputs are synthetic. Do not copy approvals, live credentials, pending jobs or enabled schedules from test to production. Promote a versioned release and validated configuration; initialize or migrate authorized production data deliberately. TESTING_AND_ROLLOUT.md specifies the checks.

### ModelProvider contract

Input: run/task/role IDs; role and playbook versions; complete task brief; source snapshot references; allowed tool schemas; expected output schema; selected model/configuration; remaining calls, tokens, time and budget.

Output: one of artifact data, tool requests, clarification request, refusal, or provider error; plus provider request ID, reported usage, latency and completion reason when available.

Implement FixtureProvider and an unconnected AnthropicProvider adapter against this interface. Configure model IDs explicitly per role category. Do not inherit a premium lead model accidentally or hard-code model names from an article. Verify current official model IDs and schema/tool support at connection time.

### Trusted tool router

### Artifact and approval invariants

An artifact version is immutable. Editing creates a new version and content hash. Approvals reference that version and exact intended use. Cross-client IDs must be rejected by repository queries and policy checks before any content is returned.

Human actors and AI role identities are different types. A synthetic demo persona is never accepted by production authentication.

An executive summary may consolidate cases but must not remove underlying critical findings or bundle unrelated permissions into one opaque approval. A human can approve one item and defer another. Show completed autonomous internal work in the activity history without turning every such completion into an approval request.

Persist environment_id on runs, artifacts, approvals, action attempts, connections and audit/cost records in addition to organization/client scope. Wrong-environment credentials or approval references must fail before execution. Company control is an operational requirement; its setup status stays pending until verified, rather than inferred from a logo or organization label.

Store concise decision rationale and visible evidence, not hidden model reasoning.

## 12. Durable workflow behavior

Use separate state machines:

- Task: planned, queued, running, awaiting_evidence, awaiting_approval, awaiting_review, completed, failed, cancelled.
- Job attempt: queued, leased, running, succeeded, retry_scheduled, failed, cancelled, outcome_unknown.
- Artifact review: draft, under_review, revision_required, reviewed, awaiting_human_decision, approved_for_defined_use, rejected, superseded.
- Action intent: proposed, authorized, executing, confirmed, failed, outcome_unknown, revoked.

An approval decision and the resulting internal job enqueue must be committed atomically. Each event/schedule occurrence has a unique key. Repeated delivery must resolve to the same business operation.

Persist checkpoints before releasing a worker for missing evidence or human review. Resume with the same run, authorization context and counters after revalidation. Waiting does not keep an AI request or worker slot occupied.

Retry only eligible transient failures, with bounded backoff and jitter. Permission denial, unsupported operations, malformed business data, missing credentials and human rejection need a visible resolution path. They must not trigger a workaround that broadens access.

### Uncertain external outcomes

Future external actions need a durable intent before dispatch, stable operation ID, provider-supported idempotency where available, and reconciliation after an ambiguous timeout or crash.

If the provider may have completed a write but acknowledgement is missing, use outcome_unknown. Reconcile by documented identifiers or escalate. Never blindly resubmit a possibly completed posting or communication. Queue locks alone cannot guarantee an exactly-once external effect.

Show cancellation truthfully. A revocation before execution prevents dispatch; a revocation after an irreversible action began cannot promise to undo it. Preserve the actual action status.

## 13. Cost, concurrency and stop controls

config/pilot_policy.example.yaml contains proposed engineering defaults:

- Maximum 2 concurrent model calls globally within an environment's application deployment, including simultaneous root runs and every worker process. Enforce shared call slots in durable state rather than a process-local semaphore. Separate environment databases do not automatically share counters.
- Maximum delegation depth 2: lead depth 0, director depth 1, specialist depth 2.
- Maximum 8 unique delegated task IDs per root run, including a critic task.
- Maximum 30 model calls per root run, counting initial calls, revisions, retries and all child tasks.
- Maximum 3 review attempts per artifact lineage, including the initial review.
- Maximum 30 minutes of active work per root run; time waiting for human decisions or missing evidence is recorded separately.
- Explicit maximum output tokens and provider-request timeouts.
- Live per-run and monthly usage budgets must be configured before enabling paid calls.

A review task reuses its ID across review attempts; each model call still counts. Retries, resumptions and revised drafts cannot reset limits. A new root run requires a distinct business assignment or explicitly approved stage, not automatic cap evasion. Organization budgets continue across all roots.

A configured schedule, new source event or resolved dependency may initiate a legitimate recurrence under an active mandate. Use a stable occurrence key and deduplicate observations. Persist the next wake condition, checkpoint and stop when no eligible work remains. Do not run the executive in an endless model-call loop or create a new root merely because a previous root hit its limits. A deterministic due-job/heartbeat check can identify stalled work without asking a model to keep checking itself. Live schedules and any outbound internal notification channel remain disabled until separately authorized and tested; the initial decision inbox is in the application.

Seven director tasks plus one critic use all eight delegated task IDs. Select relevant directors for each assignment; do not launch every role by default. The lead can integrate their findings without spawning a ninth task. Additional work can use a genuinely distinct approved stage, preserving company budget accounting.

Reserve estimated call cost atomically before dispatch and reconcile it with provider-reported usage. Count in-flight reservations across parallel tasks. Use a conservative margin because estimates and invoices can differ. Application limits supplement provider controls; do not claim an exact invoice ceiling from a prompt or estimate.

Track both gross API usage value and cash expense after included credits. Company credits are shared; a pilot workspace limit does not create a private credit wallet. The funding preference is recorded, but an unknown credit balance is not permission to spend. [S5][S6]

Prefer one active paid pilot environment initially. If staging and production both incur costs, allocate separate environment ceilings whose sum does not exceed the approved company amount, and track aggregate usage. Keep an approved allocation register and provider controls; do not pretend isolated databases provide a shared atomic spend counter. Personal contributions, if agreed, remain separately bounded without becoming the default account owner.

Provide independent controls for pause new work, stop a run, disable a schedule, revoke an approval, and disable a connector. Preserve completed work and explain the reason for every budget stop.
