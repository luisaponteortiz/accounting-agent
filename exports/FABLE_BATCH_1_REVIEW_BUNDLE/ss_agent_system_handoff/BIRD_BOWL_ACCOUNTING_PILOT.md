## Intended operating result

Luis intends to work through Bird Bowl's November and December 2026 accounting with Claude, capturing the procedures and decisions needed for an agent to continue that work consistently. The goal is dependable accounting execution that lets Theresa operate the day-to-day workflow without Luis guiding each step. Jeff can support the technical side under an agreed responsibility split, while Supporting Strategies may provide accounting co-management if Bird Bowl accepts that proposed service.

Translate the aspiration of a staff/senior accountant into a defined task-coverage plan: recurring staff-level execution, selected senior-level preparation and analysis, required review, and explicit escalation criteria. The role label is not proof that the system can replace every responsibility or judgment of a human accountant. Measure completed, verifiable work and avoidable interruptions, as well as time. Required human review remains part of successful execution.

## November–December knowledge capture and validation

This is a planned learning and documentation period, not a claim that the months have already been processed or that the agent has been built. The desired knowledge must become explicit, versioned material that the operating application can retrieve. Conversation history and repeated use of Claude alone do not establish durable model training, complete knowledge transfer, or reliable execution.

### November: capture the working process

As Luis performs the November work with Claude, document inputs, sequence, account mappings, calculations, expected outputs, review points, and exceptions. Incorporate earlier November records when available; do not assume they have already been examined. Preserve proven client logic and distinguish confirmed procedures from Claude suggestions, temporary choices, and unresolved questions.

Record each intervention Luis makes. Convert a repeatable instruction into a proposed rule, example, or workflow step; retain a genuine accounting judgment as a human decision. A correction must state whether it applies only to that transaction or establishes a broader rule. Luis validates accounting changes before they enter the approved playbook. Save the supporting evidence and original decision context.

### December: repeat, validate, and involve Theresa

Apply the documented process to new December work. Test whether the instructions are sufficient without supplying the answers used to develop them. Compare outputs with approved results, record disagreements, and revise the playbook through the same review process. Include missing information, duplicates, changed descriptions, splits, and other exceptions actually relevant to the selected workflow.

Have Theresa use the procedures during this period, explain the steps back, and practice resolving ordinary items and routing exceptions. Her feedback should identify missing context, unclear instructions, and work that still depends on Luis's intervention. Start with the assigned preparation or review responsibilities she is authorized to perform.

Complete the December close only when the necessary records and statements are available; that may require work in January 2027. November and December do not guarantee exposure to every annual, unusual, or transaction-specific case. Maintain a coverage-gap list and a clear human route for untested situations. January 1 is not an automatic deployment or readiness date.

### Portable accounting handoff

Prepare a maintained handoff containing:

- A workflow inventory stating what is covered, what remains manual, and the evidence required to complete each task.
- Current client procedures, checklists, account mappings, skills, templates, and relevant supporting scripts.
- Approved worked examples with original inputs, correct outputs, and short explanations of the treatment.
- A decision and correction register identifying scope, rationale, approver, effective version, and superseded guidance.
- A separate set of validation cases not used as teaching examples, including cases that must remain pending or require review.
- An operating guide for Theresa, plus a support guide distinguishing accounting exceptions from technical failures.

Keep authoritative accounting instructions separate from raw chats and unapproved proposals. The agent must load the applicable approved material and current work state when it runs. Preserve source references so a reviewer can trace an output to the evidence and rule used. The handoff remains limited to Bird Bowl's authorized accounting information.

Expand one workflow at a time when its documented scope and validated performance support that decision. Require completed work that can be checked against source evidence; correct identification of unresolved items; persistent progress without duplicate processing; and an exception list that explains what decision is needed. Count all supervision, review, correction, and recurring maintenance when measuring effort.

Theresa should demonstrate the assigned workflow from input through review using the operating guide, without Luis prompting each step, and explain when and how to escalate. Separately demonstrate that the proposed agent can complete its authorized portion while Luis is away from the desk, preserving the required approval boundary and continuing independent work when one item is blocked. These are readiness criteria to test, not claims of current capability.

Knowledge capture and successful tests do not authorize live connections, new permissions, posting, accounting-policy changes, spending, deployment, or an expanded client scope. Use the existing capability and human-approval gates. Confirm the actual hosting/support arrangement with Jeff and the relevant client/company authority before any operating handoff.

## Accounting application and model roles

The host runs the accounting application. The application holds its authorized connector configuration and calls supported QuickBooks operations directly. Claude is not required as an intermediary for those API requests. [S15]

Use deterministic code for established mappings/rules, calculations, validation, durable task state and supported authorized operations. Such a workflow can run without a Claude subscription/API call. Hosting and any other vendor costs remain separate.

Optional Claude assistance may interpret unfamiliar evidence, propose a category or split, analyze an exception or prepare an explanation. Its result is a proposal or structured request; the application validates scope, evidence, accounting rules and authority before any action. [S16] Missing model access sends an ambiguous item to a human exception or pending state. It must not cause guessing.

Each step states whether it is deterministic, model-assisted or human, along with its review requirement. A zero-model preparation workflow is valid only where the approved review policy permits deterministic checks plus the assigned human review. A required model review stays pending when the model is unavailable. Final close and later ledger writes retain the existing human/capability gates.

## Deployment boundary

A client profile exposes only Bird Bowl accounting preparation/execution and accounting review. Enforce the restriction in backend authorization, source retrieval, APIs and exports. Hiding company buttons in the interface is insufficient. Do not start the SS executive/director processes in the client deployment or preload SS CRM and other-client records.

The accounting pilot can run independently. A connection back to the wider SS system is optional future work requiring a defined purpose and authorized data scope. Do not build that dependency merely because the broader company architecture exists.

Before optional client hosting, identify with Jeff the platform and its runtime/job capabilities, persistent storage, authentication, secrets handling, backups, administrators and cost arrangements. Confirm company/client authority and the scope of support. Do not copy shared SS credentials into the client's environment by default. Decide separately who supplies and pays for optional model usage.

Use the common local-to-test-to-production verification sequence, with separate credentials, evidence, approvals and queues. Account selection, permission grants, spending and live processing remain inactive until authorized.
